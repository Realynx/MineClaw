# Modular mine layouts

The Mine Designer offers six reusable excavation pieces: a narrow exploration tunnel, a branch network, a chamber, a straight stair descent, a spiral shaft and a four-way junction. Click a card and then the mine, or drag its card onto the plot. The pointer projects onto the selected **entrance feet Y**, independently of the display cutaway. Pieces are centred around the pointer rather than growing entirely toward one edge.

Select a piece to rotate, resize, move or remove it. Dimensions commit on blur or Enter so typing a new number does not repeatedly rebuild the mine. Nearby opposing entrances on the same feet level snap to adjacent cells; the snapping does not silently change the chosen height. A piece must fit completely inside the 64 × 64 plot and Y1–127 and may not excavate bedrock. Placement refuses invalid geometry instead of clipping it.

## Selecting and navigating

The **Components** outliner stays at the top right, above the scrolling properties and layout palette. It lists existing layout pieces with their coordinates. Click a row to select it through terrain, then use its axis handles, arrow keys / PgUp / PgDn, or the inspector's X/Y/Z fields to move it. Selecting another component reveals its inspector without scrolling the page. Coordinate edits commit on blur or Enter; Escape cancels the draft. Double-click a row or use the frame button to centre the camera on the whole component. With the canvas focused, **F** or **Numpad .** frames the selection.

Mine Designer keeps the cursor visible. **Middle-drag orbits**, **Shift + middle-drag pans**, and the **wheel zooms** around the focus point. **Alt + left-drag** and **Shift + Alt + left-drag** offer the same orbit and pan gestures without a middle button. Plain left clicks select components or use the selected excavation tool; missing a movement handle never enters immersive freecam. Handles keep a readable screen size while zooming. Builder retains its click-to-lock, WASD freecam controls.

The list represents layout components created with the palette, including locally recovered saved components. Manual excavation and older cell-only presets remain editable cells rather than guessed component groups.

## Layout choices and evidence

The palette separates extraction from access. Narrow tunnels expose fresh rock at low excavation cost; branch networks organize that exploration around a shared corridor. Chambers and junctions are connection and working space, not yield optimizations. Straight stairs and compact spirals provide stepped access with three blocks of clearance. The defaults leave a solid supporting block under each descent path cell.

The project's [mine layout study](mine-blueprints.md) remains the source for the default four-solid-block branch gap and its beta/1.12 ore targets. Its measured results apply to MineClaw's own terrain simulator, not an exact reproduction of a vanilla seed. A gap counts the solid blocks between branches: four means a branch every fifth block. Small gaps trade more excavation for more coverage. The palette exposes that setting instead of implying one universally optimal pattern.

Research checked on 2026-11-08:

- Philip Golden's original [Mining with the Agent: Part II](https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/lessons/mining-with-the-agent-part-ii), hosted by Minecraft Education and updated June 2018, explicitly compares straight and spiral staircase shafts, proposes branch patterns for finding grouped ores and suggests an underground crafting base to reduce repeated surface trips. This supports offering distinct access, extraction and hub pieces rather than one oversized mining form. The associated ore chart is used as a planning aid, not a guarantee of yield.
- Minecraft Education's [Coding with Minecraft 7: Iteration student workbook](https://education.minecraft.net/content/dam/education-edition/learning-experiences/coding_with_minecraft/Coding%20with%20Minecraft%207-Iteration-Student%20workbook.pdf), page 23, illustrates a spiral descent and three-block column removal at each turn. Its preceding straight-stair exercise discusses widening the passage and retaining headroom. The palette adopts those geometry families, while its exact dimensions and supporting-floor checks come from the local generators and regression tests.
- Mojang's [Java Edition 1.18 release notes](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/4415128577293-Minecraft-Java-Edition-1-18) explicitly describe changed world height and ore distribution. This is why modern negative-Y advice must not replace the existing beta 1.8.1 and 1.12.2 tables. The designer keeps those versions' practical feet targets: Y11 for diamond/redstone/gold, Y16 for lapis/iron and Y48 for coal. These practical targets are not all density peaks.
- Samomezz's [original mining simulation repository](https://github.com/samomezz/minecraftStripMiningSimulation) publishes code, data and limitations for a spacing study across 14 worlds. It samples only four vertical blocks around modern Y−59/−58 and reports a plateau around five to six solid blocks. Its version, height and limited vein sampling differ from this project's study, so it supports adjustable gaps and a coverage/effort tradeoff, not replacing the local four-block default or old-version heights.

The coloured ore-height chart remains visible and can collapse. Ore chips set entrance height; the chart and cutaway slider change the view only. Planned excavation appears in cyan. Amber outlines show each entire face-connected ore vein discovered in or directly beside that excavation, including visible floor ore and segments near hazards. Unrelated seed caves and ore remain hidden, and authored blocks replace the ore underneath them. This is a discovery preview, not permission or a guarantee to excavate every outlined cell: miners still protect access floors and supports and apply their normal ownership and hazard checks. Yield estimates are illustrative; miners may follow eligible connected ore beyond the authored excavation.

## State, overlap and undo

`mine/modules.ts` produces ordinary authored excavation cells, entrance ports and snapping. `MineModuleController` keeps the instances and each covered cell's previous authored state. Moving or removing a piece recomputes the union of the remaining pieces. Shared tunnels remain, older manual excavation is restored and subsequent manual exceptions such as a torch or a filled block are retained. The mine canvas distinguishes untouched natural air from explicitly authored excavation so tunnels crossing caves remain part of the plan.

Studio history attaches undo/redo metadata to the same operation as the cells, including a completely overlapping piece that changes no cells. Repeated moves with one gesture identifier coalesce into one undo step. A move combined with changed dimensions is committed atomically.

Instance metadata is saved locally per owner/session/version/seed. After a successful design save, it is also copied under that design revision, allowing a new designer session in the same browser to recover movable pieces. Recovery requires every owned cell to match the loaded canvas (including manual exceptions); stale metadata is discarded and never changes the canvas. The server stores the ordinary excavation and placed cells. Piece grouping is currently browser-local and does not travel to another device. Existing saved mines and version presets remain usable as ordinary canvas cells without inferred groups.

Regression coverage in `mineModules.test.ts` checks all six layouts and rotations, entrance clearance, descent floors, compatible snapping, bounds refusal, overlaps, manual edits, metadata-only undo, grouped drags and validated metadata recovery. `studioMine.test.tsx` covers the palette, drag payloads, separate entrance/view heights and the version-specific guide.

## Saving and recovery (updated 2026-11-10)

This supersedes the "browser-local" note above: the component layout is saved with each design revision (`mine_authoring`, at most 2 000 000 characters; a larger layout is refused before anything is sent) and reopens on any browser. The browser keeps a working copy as well, so unsaved component work survives a reload in the same browser.

- **Edits reach the bot in order.** Canvas edits go to the bot one request at a time per session, in edit order, each under a 200 000-byte budget (`DESIGNER_CANVAS_BATCH_BYTES`; the bridge and the bot accept 256 KiB). A big edit is split by bytes. A 413 halves the budget; 429, 409 (the bot's "session is busy"), 408, 423, 425, 5xx and network errors retry after 2, 4, 8 … 30 s; any other 4xx stops the sync until Retry. Leaving the page sends edits still waiting for the debounce once. The anchor, a chat message and a save wait in the same line. A torch correction from the bot is dropped when a newer edit to that cell is still waiting.
- **Save never runs ahead of unsent edits.** Save, send and the kind, seed, version and preset changes wait for the edits to arrive and refuse with "The latest edits have not reached the designer (…)" when they cannot. A hidden or closing page sends at once.
- **What the header shows.** A sync chip ("Synced", "Sending N cells", "Retrying: …", "Not syncing: … · Retry"), an "Unsaved changes" chip and a primary Save while dirty, and "edited since" on the saved bar. Ctrl+S saves. A session this browser saved at an older canvas rev reopens dirty. A failed picture no longer stops a save. After a save the page compares the bot's canvas with its own and offers "Resync and save again" when cells differ. Errors and the restore note stay until dismissed.
- **Unsaved work in an earlier session.** "Open in studio" (`?id=N`) starts a new session, so this browser remembers, per design, the last session it left with unsaved changes (`mineclaw.studio.unsaved:<owner>:<design>`). Opening the design again offers "Resume it", which reopens that session (`?session=S&id=N`). The entry goes once that session is clean.
- **Restore keeps what still matches.** The working copy (key `mineclaw.mine-modules.v2:<owner>:<session>`, with a `descends` fingerprint of the server layout it came from; the older v1 key is read as a fallback) wins when it descends from the server's layout and fits; otherwise the server's layout, then other local copies. Components whose cells no longer match the excavation are dropped, the rest are restored, and a note says how many were dropped. The canvas is never changed by a restore. Working copies are pruned to 20 sessions per owner; a session "Open in studio" offers to resume always keeps its copy, and the copies kept per saved revision are never pruned (for a design saved before the server kept layouts they are the only record).

## Editing (added 2026-11-10)

- **Moving.** Dragging a selected component's handle moves only its outline; the release commits one move (one undo step). Esc, a lost pointer or a lost window puts it back. Arrow presses in one animation frame are added into one move, and presses less than 600 ms apart share an undo step, so a held key is one step. The X-ray plan and the dig estimate update 200 ms after the canvas stops changing, never during a gesture.
- **Dragging a piece by its body.** A press on a component selects it; past a few pixels the drag moves its outline across its own level and snaps it to the nearest opening of another piece (within 2 blocks), and the release is one move. Esc puts it back. The handles still move it up and down.
- **Openings.** While a piece is placed or moved, the cells where another piece can join are marked in amber on the floor, and the ones the outline joins there in green.
- **A piece in hand.** [ ], R and Shift+R turn it where the pointer is; right-click or Esc puts it down; Delete does nothing, and nor do the arrows and PgUp/PgDn while something is selected. A refused placement or move keeps its outline, drawn red, and the tooltip gives the reason.
- **Selections in a mine.** Delete removes the plan inside the selection (fills dug cells back, removes placed blocks) and never digs: digging stays with the Box tool. Esc clears the selection, and a click without a drag selects nothing. With components, the whole-design turn and mirror are hidden, and a raw move, turn or mirror over component cells is refused with "Select the component to move it."
- **Components.** Ctrl+G combines the pieces ticked under Combine (Ctrl-click a row in the outliner to tick or untick it, Shift-click to tick a range); a newly combined group turns about the centre of its footprint (existing groups keep their anchor). Ungroup (the inspector button or Ctrl+Shift+G) puts the parts back in the group's place in the list, where they stood. Every piece can be named; the inspector shows "N cells to dig"; each outliner row has Duplicate and Delete. Combined components are not reusable across designs yet.
- **Levels and view.** "Work at Y n" is the fixed placement Y; setting it (ore chips, the Entrance Y field, PgUp/PgDn with nothing selected, Shift ×5) moves the view cut to Y + 2. Top view and Frame all are buttons. Paste goes to the working level; Duplicate keeps the original's height.
- **Starting.** "New mine" on the Designs page opens `/designs/studio?kind=mine`. An empty mine shows three start steps and the presets at the top of the panel.
- **Keys.** ? (or the ? button by the view) opens the shortcut sheet (`StudioShortcuts.tsx`). The hint line under the view follows the state: nothing selected, a component selected, a box selected, or a piece in hand.

## Queuing a mine in Story Mode (added 2026-11-10)

"Build in Controller" picks the saved mine up at its absolute y. The Controller queues it in parts: one `story.builds` add per `mine` item, in order (the dig top-down, then the blocks put in), all with the design's `group`, and AI bodies in the selection get one directive with the first part (each directive is a brain turn, and a newer build directive replaces the last). A refused directive is reported on its own and does not stop the colony's parts. An item closes at 4 096 cells or about 56 000 bytes of cell JSON, so each request stays under the supervisor's 64 KiB Story body cap at any coordinates. The first failure stops the rest and says "queued N of M parts". A mine that would need more than 64 parts is refused rather than cut short.

Further coverage: `studioSync.test.ts` (the request line, caps, retries, late corrections), `mineAuthoringRoundTrip.test.ts` with the shared fixture `contracts/test/fixtures/mine-authoring-nested.json` (also read by the contracts, API and bot tests), `studioSave.test.ts` and `studioChrome.test.tsx` (save flow, chips, notices), `mineReadouts.test.ts`, `studioMineSafety.test.ts`, `studioNewMine.test.tsx`, `studioShortcuts.test.tsx`, `studioPageWiring.test.tsx` (the whole studio page with only the 3D viewer replaced: sync, save, keys and panel wiring), and in `studioMine.test.tsx` the parted queue and the torch pick-up.

## Shared component machinery (added 2026-11-10)

The redstone designer's components (docs/design/redstone-designer.md, "Components as movable parts") move the same way as mine pieces, so the interaction now lives in `features/studio/components/` and both designers use it. The two controllers stay separate: mine cells are generated from a spec, redstone cells come from a layout and remember what they covered. Mine behaviour did not change, and its tests pass unchanged.

- **`levelRay.ts`.** `rayFromPointer` (the camera ray under a client point), `levelPoint` (the cell where a ray meets a canvas level; it subtracts the plot's base, 0 in a mine and 64 on a build or redstone plot), `rayBox` and `nearestHit` (the item whose box a ray meets first, boxes in canvas cells). `mine/placementRay.ts` keeps its level-range check and calls these.
- **`dragSession.ts`.** `ComponentDrag`, a framework-free drag of one component: a press on it becomes a drag past 5 px, the pointer moves are previewed through a scheduler (once per animation frame by default; the mine passes `immediately`, so it still previews on each pointer move as before), and the release commits once, or cancels when the piece is back where it began. Esc, a cancelled pointer, a lost pointer capture, the window losing focus and unbinding each put the piece back. Its window listeners are capture phase. A new press cancels a drag still held.
- **`outline.ts`.** `prepareOutline` works out a piece's outline once per drag, relative to its corner, so each frame only moves it (a plain box past the outline cell limit). `placementPreview.ts` uses it.
- **`ComponentOutliner.tsx`.** The outliner taken out of `MineComponents.tsx`: rows, selection, Ctrl- and Shift-click ticks, the Combine checklist, keyboard navigation, F and double-click to frame, and per-row action buttons (`OutlinerRowAction`). `MineComponents` is now a thin wrapper that passes its rows, glyphs, labels and test ids, so the DOM and the accessible names are unchanged.
- **Ports.** The overlay's `setComponentPorts(open, joined)` draws the amber and green openings for any component kind; `setMinePorts` stays as its alias.

**Dragging by the body, the mouse free.** A mine never locks the mouse. With the Select tool, a plain left press on a piece selects it, and past a few pixels the drag moves its outline across its own level, snapped to the nearest opening; Esc puts it back; the release is one move and one undo step. A press on a handle is the handle's. Alt + left-drag and the middle button stay with the camera (orbit, Shift to pan), over a piece or over empty space alike. `web/apps/web/e2e/studio-mine.spec.ts` now covers this on a combined preset: the outliner's selected row, Esc, a release and its undo, and an Alt-drag that orbits.
